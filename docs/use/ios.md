# The iOS app

The iOS app connects to your Squirmy server, browses and searches the library, and plays it as part of your shared playback session. This page covers getting the app, connecting it, what it does, and what is not finished.

## Getting the app

If you have a TestFlight invitation, open it on your iPhone or iPad and install Squirmy in TestFlight once a build is available. You can also build the app from the repository on a Mac with Xcode. The app is in `apps/mobile` and uses Expo SDK 58. The system Liquid Glass tab bar and mini-player need iOS 26 or later.

You need Node.js 22.13 or newer. Install from the repository root, which also applies the patch in `patches/` that the app depends on:

```sh
git clone https://github.com/IsaacThoman/squirmy.git
cd squirmy
npm ci
cd apps/mobile
```

Then pick one:

- **Try it in a simulator.** Start Expo and press `i` to open it in the iOS Simulator in an SDK 58-compatible Expo Go:

  ```sh
  npx expo start --go
  ```

- **Build the standalone app** in Xcode and run it in the simulator:

  ```sh
  npm run ios
  ```

- **Install it on your iPhone.** Connect the phone, then build for it in the Release configuration:

  ```sh
  npx expo run:ios --device --configuration Release
  ```

  Expo generates the native project in `apps/mobile/ios/`. To sign with your own Apple developer team, open `ios/Squirmy.xcworkspace` and set your team and your own bundle identifier under **Signing & Capabilities**. The default bundle identifier in `app.json` is `com.isaacthoman.squirmy`.

For working on the app itself, the [README](https://github.com/IsaacThoman/squirmy#mobile-app) describes the **Squirmy Dev** development build, which installs next to the regular app and loads code from Metro.

## Connecting to your server

1. On first launch, **Connect to your server** asks for its address. Type it as you would in a browser: `music.example.com`, or `http://192.168.1.20:4747` on your home network. Without `http://` or `https://`, the app tries HTTPS first and then HTTP.
2. Choose **Continue**. The first time it reaches a server on your local network, iOS asks to allow Local Network access; allow it.
3. Sign in with your username and password, or create an account with an invite code from your admin.

If the server has not been set up, the app says so, shows which version of Squirmy it runs, and offers **Open in Browser** to create the admin account. On a public address over plain HTTP, the sign-in screen warns that your password is sent unencrypted; see [Reverse proxies and HTTPS](../server/reverse-proxy.md). For connection errors, see [Troubleshooting](../server/troubleshooting.md#the-app-cannot-connect).

The sign-in screen and the account sheet show the server's address and its Squirmy version, as the server reports it now: a release number such as `1.4.0`, or `dev` (with the commit, for example `dev+1a2b3c4`) for a server run from a checkout or an image built without a version.

The app keeps your sign-in in the iPhone's keychain. If the keychain refuses it (a build without the keychain entitlement, for instance), the app still works until you quit it, and says once that you will need to sign in again next time.

To change servers or sign out, open the account sheet and choose **Switch server** or **Sign out**.

## Using it

The app has Home, Library (playlists, albums, artists and songs: the music on your server plus what you added, not songs fetched only because you played them), and Search, with album, artist and playlist pages, and Now Playing with lyrics and the queue. The library is cached on the phone, so it opens at once and stays browsable while offline. Without a connection, downloaded music plays, and so does what the streaming cache holds.

- **Playback is your shared session.** Whatever plays in the web app or on another device shows here too, and the phone becomes a remote without sound of its own. The playback device button is highlighted when playback is elsewhere. In the mini-player or Now Playing, it opens **Playback device**: move playback to another device, and set the playing device's volume. Pressing play when nothing is playing makes this phone the player. Only devices that are open and responding are listed; when the one playing drops off, the **Playback device** sheet shows **Reconnecting to …** for up to 20 seconds, then playback pauses. The phone itself leaves your other devices' lists when you switch away from the app with nothing playing, and is back when you open it; while it plays in the background it stays listed. See [Devices and transferring playback](../server/playback.md#devices-and-transferring-playback).
- **Volume is the phone's own.** The slider in Now Playing, and in **Playback device** while this phone plays, is the system volume: the side buttons and Control Center move it too. To send this phone's sound to AirPlay or Bluetooth, open **Playback device** and tap **AirPlay and Bluetooth** (shown while this phone plays), or use Control Center. The app has no volume of its own on top (only volume normalization, if on). Other devices see this phone's real level, and when one of them changes it while the phone plays, the phone's volume moves to match (iOS shows its volume indicator). While another device plays, the slider sets that device's volume. Like Apple Music's, the sliders have no knob: drag anywhere along the bar, and it thickens while you drag. The iOS Simulator has no system volume, so the slider is blank there.
- **Background audio** keeps playing with the app closed, with lock screen and Control Center controls. The star beside them, as in Apple Music, is the song's heart: it is filled when the song is in your library, and tapping it saves or removes the song, as the heart in the app does. It shows once the song's details have loaded.
- **Like from the mini-player.** The heart between the playback device button and play/pause saves the playing song to your library or removes it, the same as the heart in Now Playing. It shows once the song's details have loaded, and not in the smaller mini-player beside a shrunken tab bar.
- **Swipe the mini-player to skip.** Swipe it left for the next song or right for the one before: the title slides with your finger and the other song's comes in after it. Let go before it is far enough and it springs back. Right always goes back a song, however far into this one you are; at the start or end of the queue (with Autoplay off) the title only gives a little. With Reduce Motion on, the title cross-fades instead. VoiceOver offers **Next track** and **Previous track** as actions on the mini-player.
- **Now Playing.** Tap the mini-player, or pull it up, to expand it. The artwork sits right under the grabber, as in Apple Music. Drag down across the artwork, the song details or the controls to collapse it. Now Playing reopens on the pane you last selected. Swipe left or right to skip. A long title, or a credit with featured artists, wraps to a second line. Tap the title or artist for **Go to album** and **Go to artist** (one for each credited artist), each naming where it leads; choosing one collapses Now Playing and opens the page. Drag along the progress bar to seek: it moves by as far as your finger does, from where it was, so touching it never jumps and a tap does nothing. Past either end the bar stretches a little and springs back, with a light tap as you reach the end. The times grow and brighten while you drag (with Reduce Motion they only brighten), and the song jumps when you let go. While the server is still getting the song, the bar stays where you let go and the song starts from there. A drag that starts up or down leaves the position alone. **Lyrics** follow the song, with the line being sung bright near the top and the others dimmed and blurred. Tap a line to jump to it. While a song plays, the controls fade away after a few seconds so the lyrics fill the screen; tap the lyrics to bring them back. Scroll to read ahead or back: every line comes into focus, and a few seconds after you stop, the lyrics return to the line being sung (a tap returns at once). When the lyrics name their writers, a **Written By** line follows the last one. Plain lyrics, without timing, keep the controls on screen and stay where you scroll them. With VoiceOver on, the controls stay; with Reduce Motion or Reduce Transparency on, the lines are dimmed but not blurred. **Lyrics** shows a spinner while they load, says **No lyrics available** (or **Instrumental**) when the song has none, and **Couldn’t load lyrics** with **Try again** when the request failed.
- **Queue.** It is laid out like Apple Music's. It opens on the song playing, with **Shuffle**, **Repeat** and **Autoplay** under it, filled while they are on. Then comes **Continue Playing**, which says where its songs come from (**Up Next** once you've added songs of your own, with how many there are), and the **Autoplay** suggestions: with Autoplay on, they're there from the start, so you can see what plays after your queue. Scroll and the heading of the part you're in stays at the top, under the toggles. Your **History** is above the song playing. Let go partway into it and the queue settles back on the song playing, or a full screen into your history. Opening the queue again puts it back on the song playing. **Clear**, beside the heading of the songs coming up, empties them at once, as on the web (the History's **Clear** asks first). Tap a song to jump to it (the songs above it are skipped, except those you added yourself). To reorder, drag a song by the handle at the end of its row, or hold the row and drag. Tap a song's **⋯** for its menu: **Save to library**, **Play next**, **Move up**, **Move down**, **Remove from queue** and the rest. In a queued song's menu, **Play now** plays it where it is, and **Play next** and **Move to end** move it rather than adding it a second time; for an Autoplay suggestion, **Play next** and **Add to queue** move it into your queue. How Previous, Shuffle and Repeat treat the list a song was played from is in [The queue](../server/playback.md#the-queue). The queue holds up to 10,000 songs; adding more than fit does nothing. A song whose details haven't loaded says **Loading…**, or offline **Song not loaded**, and its menu only moves it or takes it out of the queue until they arrive.
- **Search** shows one list as you type, every kind mixed (artists with round pictures, albums, songs and your playlists, each saying what it is: "Album · MGMT"), ordered by how well the name matches and how well known it is, with your own music and what you play ranked higher. **All**, **Songs**, **Albums**, **Artists** and **Playlists** at the top narrow it to one kind, which loads more as you scroll; at the largest text sizes they are a menu under the search field instead. A song plays, with the songs found after it in the queue; a gray song you can't play opens its album; an artist, album or playlist opens. Hold a row, or tap its **⋯**, for its menu: a song's usual menu, an album's **Play**, **Shuffle**, **Save to library** (or **Remove from library**) and **Go to artist**, an artist's **Start radio** and **Save to library**. The last results stay on screen until the new ones arrive, so the list doesn't empty as you type, and scrolling hides the keyboard. Before you type, Search shows **Recently viewed** results you opened or played from Search, then genres. Tap **Clear all** to clear the list. Recently viewed results stay on the phone, for your account on that server.
- **Back to where you browsed.** The back button on an album, artist, or other collection names the previous screen when it can, such as **Search** or **Artist**. It brings you back to the same screen in the tab. When a collection zooms open from its cover, it zooms back to that cover when you tap Back, pull down from the top of the page, or swipe right to go back.
- **Your library** is what you heart, plus the music stored on your server. The heart is the one control: on a song, on an album page and on an artist page, it saves that to your library (a song also joins **Liked Songs**, and a saved artist appears in Library), and tapping it again removes it. The heart itself is the confirmation; to take a change back, tap it again. If the server refuses one, the heart goes back to how it was and shows a red exclamation mark for a few seconds (the song's menu says **Couldn’t save to library. Try again**); tap it to try again. A heart made offline waits for the connection, which isn't a failure. Playing or downloading never adds anything. Music stored on the server is always in everyone's library; its heart only shows whether you saved it yourself. Until you save something, Library invites you to find music in Search. Library shows Liked Songs, your playlists, your albums and your artists together in one grid (artists with round pictures), in **Recently played** order: what you last started playing from on any device comes first, then everything you haven't played, most recently added first. It moves as you play on your other devices. An album, playlist or artist is played when you press its **Play** or **Shuffle** or a song on its page; a song started from Search, Home, a radio or your history doesn't count for its album, and the next song, Autoplay, Next, Previous or resuming change nothing ([What counts as played](./web.md#what-counts-as-played)). You can sort by **Recently added**, **Title**, **Artist** or **Year**. Hold an album, artist, playlist or Liked Songs in Library, or tap a row's **⋯**, and choose **Pin to top** to keep it at the top whatever the sort, most recently pinned first, with a pin before its subtitle. Pins are the same on the web and your other devices; **Unpin** puts it back in its place. Tap the list button to switch between a grid and a list, or the plus button to create a playlist. The Library title and its controls scroll with your collections. **Find in your library** finds only what the Library lists: albums and songs fetched just because you played them stay out (on a server whose library holds more than 2,000 albums, such albums can still show up in the search).
- **Downloads.** The download button on an album, a playlist or Liked Songs keeps it on the phone, and a song's menu has **Download**. Downloaded music always plays from the phone. Everything you can play downloads, also songs the server doesn't have yet: it gets them first, and each downloads as soon as it is there, so a download can take longer than its size suggests. The line under the buttons counts every song (**Downloading 2 of 3**) and says **Downloaded** only once all of them are on the phone. A song the server can't get is counted as **not available** (**2 of 3 downloaded · 1 not available**), with **Retry** to ask again. Without **Can fetch missing music**, only songs stored on the server download, and the rest are counted the same way. **Downloads**, in the Library header and the account sheet, shows what is kept and sets the download quality, Wi-Fi only and a storage limit.
- **Streaming cache.** The app keeps the songs you played most recently on the phone, and gets the next two in your queue ahead of time. They start at once, keep playing when the connection drops, and use no data the second time. It is separate from Downloads: you choose what to download, and downloads stay until you remove them, while the cache fills by itself and removes the songs played longest ago to stay under its limit. On Wi-Fi the song you are streaming is kept too. On cellular only the songs coming up are kept, at your **Cellular** quality, and nothing is when that quality is **Data Saver**. Signing out or switching server empties it (a session that expired keeps it for when you sign in again), and iOS may empty it when the phone runs short of storage.
- **Tap the song that's playing** in a list to pause it, and again to resume, rather than starting it over.
- **Every song has a menu**: play next or last, **Save to library** or **Remove from library** (the same as its heart), add to a playlist, start a radio (from songs the server plays for you), go to its album or to each of its artists. An artist page says what tells the artist apart from others of the same name ("60s band from the UK"), then shows Top Songs, each release once grouped by kind (Albums, EPs & Singles, Compilations, Live Albums, Soundtracks, Other Releases), releases by other artists that feature them under **Appears On** (with their year), similar artists, and last **About**, three lines of the biography with **More** for the rest; it has a radio button when something by the artist plays for you. A heading with more than 20 releases, and Top Songs or a Home song row with more than 5 songs, has **See all**. An album page lists its songs first, each with its length (– when the server doesn't know it), numbering each disc separately under its own heading, then the release date and label, then any description; explicit songs are marked **E**. While the server is still looking up an album's songs, the page says **Looking up the songs…** and **Play** can be pressed already: it starts once they arrive. After a minute it says **Still looking for the songs** with **Check again** (pulling the page down checks again too); an album whose songs the server doesn't know says **No songs listed**. Play and Shuffle stay in place, dimmed while there is nothing to play. Hold an album, playlist or artist cover on Home, in Library or on an artist page for its menu instead of opening it: an album's **Play**, **Shuffle** and **Go to artist**, or **Open**; in Library also **Pin to top** or **Unpin**. Songs you can't play can be added to your [wantlist](./wantlist.md), when your server has it on.
- **Playlists.** **Add to playlist** in a song's menu lists your playlists, or makes a new one (names can be up to 120 characters). The sheet waits for the server, with a spinner on the playlist you chose, and closes once the song is in it; if it couldn't be added, the sheet says so and stays open to try again. A playlist that already has the song doesn't get it twice unless you choose **Add Anyway**: the app asks first, or, for a playlist you haven't opened on the phone, as soon as the server says it is already there. Removing a song from a playlist takes it out at once, with no Undo: add it again from its menu. If the server refuses a removal, a move or a deletion, the playlist goes back to how it was and its page says so under the header.
- **No pop-up messages.** What you do shows where you did it: the heart fills, the queue or the playlist changes, the radio starts. When something fails it says so in the same place, and VoiceOver reads it out: a radio that didn't start on its button (**Couldn’t start radio. Try again**), **Play** or **Shuffle** that could only load part of a long list under the buttons (it plays what it loaded), a wantlist change on the album's button, a settings change the server refused or a scrobbling service's error under its controls in Settings (a section that couldn't load says so, with **Try Again**), and a sign-in the phone couldn't keep (**This iPhone couldn’t save your sign-in**) on the account sheet. What playback has to say (a song skipped or that couldn't play, a pause because the server can't fetch music, the phone playing with your other devices again, a song queued from a menu or a swipe (**Playing next** or **Added to queue**, with a tap), a radio from a menu on its way (**Starting radio…**) or one that didn't start) shows in the mini-player and in Now Playing where the artist would be: for a few seconds, or, when playback paused because of it, until you press play or move to another song.
- **Changes on your other devices show here as they happen**: hearts, what they add to or remove from your library, and playlists. A playlist deleted elsewhere closes with **Couldn’t load this playlist**, even when it is downloaded.
- **Settings**, from the account sheet: **Volume Normalization**, **Streaming Quality** on **Wi-Fi** and on **Cellular**, **Storage**, and scrobbling to [ListenBrainz and Last.fm](../server/scrobbling.md). Under **Storage**, **Streaming Cache** sets the cache's size limit (**Off**, 1, 2, 5 or 10 GB; 2 GB to begin with), **Used** shows what it holds now, and **Clear** empties it after asking (those songs no longer play without a connection; downloads stay); it starts filling again when the queue moves on. A lower limit takes effect at once. The limit belongs to the phone, not to your account.
- **Now Playing on iPad.** The artwork fills the width of the window, with the song's title and the playback controls right under it. In a wide enough window, **Lyrics** and **Queue** slide in as a pane on the right, and the artwork and controls move over beside it. Tap **Lyrics** or **Queue** again, or swipe the pane to the right, to put it away. In a narrow window, such as a small Split View, Lyrics and Queue look as they do on iPhone.
- **Larger text.** The app follows the system text size. Sheets that grow taller than the screen scroll, so **Sign out** and the device actions stay in reach. Song rows grow with the text (on iOS 18 and later), and at the accessibility sizes a song's title and artist wrap and an album song's length moves under its title; queue rows grow the same way. At the accessibility sizes, rows of covers on Home and artist pages show wider covers so titles wrap into whole words, grids of covers have two columns, and one from the third accessibility size. At the accessibility sizes, Settings puts each choice under its name and sign-in stacks the server's name and address; a server address stays on one line, shrunk to fit. From **xxLarge** up, the mini-player shows the song's title alone, larger, instead of two smaller lines, except when it needs to show a playback notice.

## Playing separately on one device

To have the phone play its own music while your other devices play something else (two people on one account, or two rooms), open **Playback device** and choose **Play separately on this iPhone**. It happens at once: if the phone was playing it carries on and your other devices pause where they were; otherwise it starts from a paused copy of the queue. While it plays separately, the highlighted device button uses a crossed-out link. In Now Playing it is labeled **Only on this device**. Open **Playback device** to see:

- **Rejoin your other devices**: the phone's own queue is cleared and it shows what your other devices play.
- **Bring this queue with me**: your other devices switch to what the phone plays, and the phone keeps playing.

Every device lists one playing like this under **Playing separately**, with the song it plays, and playback can't be moved to it. Playing separately survives a relaunch; after a week away the phone plays with your other devices again, and the mini-player says so.

## On-demand songs

When an admin has [connected a provider](../server/acquisition.md), songs that aren't on the server yet look and play like any other: nothing marks where a song's file is. Play one and the play button and its row show the same spinner as buffering while the server gets it, and the artist stays under the title; after eight seconds the current song's subtitle says **Still loading…**. Songs further up the queue show nothing special. If the source doesn't have the song playing or the next one, the mini-player and Now Playing say **Skipped “Title” — not available** where the artist would be (or **Skipped 3 songs — not available** when several go in a row), playback moves on to the next song it can play, and the song shows gray with **Not available**. When none of the songs left in the queue are available, playback stops and the player says **None of the remaining songs are available**. If five songs in a row aren't available, playback pauses with **Several songs in a row aren’t available**; press play to carry on. When the server can't fetch music at all right now (its source is down), it pauses at the first song, and the player says **Can’t load music right now** and why until you press play to try again. A song that fails for another reason says **Couldn’t play “Title”**, and after several of those in a row the server pauses the same way. A song you tapped by name is different: playback stays on it, paused, and the player says **Couldn’t play “Title”** (with **It isn’t available.** when the source doesn't have it) until you press play or move on. Members need **Can fetch missing music** for this; without it, those songs are gray (**Not available**), an album with some of them says how many songs are available, and their menu offers the wantlist instead when the server has it on. Offline, songs that would play with a connection say **Not downloaded**.

## Known limits

- **Gapless playback is close but not sample-accurate.** The next track starts as the previous one ends, which can leave a tiny seam on continuous albums.
- **Lock screen and Control Center controls** are implemented but have not yet been verified on a physical device.
- **Volume normalization only turns loud tracks down.** It never boosts quiet ones on iOS.
- **A paused phone drops off other devices' lists** once iOS suspends the app, and comes back when you open it.
- **The streaming cache does not know about Low Data Mode.** It follows the kind of network and your **Cellular** quality only. Choose **Data Saver** for cellular, or set the cache to **Off**, to stop it using cellular data.
- **Albums and artists stay gray offline unless something of theirs is downloaded**, even when the cache holds some of their songs. The songs themselves are lit wherever they are listed.
- **Android** keeps compiling but is not a focus. On Android, use a [Subsonic client](../server/subsonic.md).
